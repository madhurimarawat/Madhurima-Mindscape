The Beauty of Having Too Many Interests

November 12, 2026

Have you ever been interested in so many things that you genuinely cannot remember all of them? One day, you are fascinated by programming. The next day, you are reading about psychology. Then suddenly, you want to explore graphic design, learn something about science, read a complicated poem, listen to music, and write a blog about all of it.

Welcome to my world!

Before we begin, let me clarify something. Having too many interests does not mean that you are interested in everything all the time. It means that you have several interests that keep returning to your life. Some stay with you for years. Others appear suddenly, become your entire personality for a few weeks, and then quietly disappear, only to return when you least expect them.

And since the name of my blog is Madhurima Mindscape, I suppose this is the perfect opportunity to talk about myself. Ha ha! A little narcissistic, perhaps? I have read a little psychology, so I know that narcissism is associated with excessive self-focus and self-importance. Don't worry, this is simply an article about my interests, not a clinical assessment of my personality!

So, what is the beauty of having too many interests? Well, I think it is both a blessing and a curse.

The blessing is that there are so many things in this world that can make you curious. The curse is that sometimes you have absolutely no idea which one deserves your attention first.

"Some people have a calling. I seem to have an entire call centre of interests, and every single one wants my attention."

1. The Never-Ending Curiosity: Why Does Everything Feel Interesting?

Let me give you a small example of how my mind works.

I wake up and think, "Oh, I should make a new website!" Then another thought appears: "Wait, I have some data that needs cleaning. Maybe I should work on that." And suddenly, I remember that I absolutely love beautiful graphs. I want to create some cute diagrams, explore different visualisations, and perhaps make a colourful pie chart.

And if you know me, you know that I absolutely love pastel gradients. Soft colours, beautiful combinations, and those lovely shades that somehow make even an ordinary chart look interesting. Why have a plain graph when you can have a beautiful one?

Somewhere in between, another question appears: "Am I really curious about everything, or am I simply incapable of choosing one thing?"

I think human beings are naturally curious. Whenever we encounter something unfamiliar, there is often an urge to explore it, understand how it works, and discover what we can learn from it.

And that curiosity can take us in so many different directions. Let me tell you about some of mine.

Programming: My Tech-Side Curiosity

Programming is one of my long-standing interests. I studied Computer Science and Engineering during my B.Tech, and naturally, I ended up working on several projects.

If you visit my GitHub profile, you will find that my projects are not all of the same kind. There are websites, machine learning projects, services, frontend experiments, and several other things.

And then there are notes and resources. I have this habit of collecting useful links, tutorials, notes, and references. At some point, I look at everything I have collected and think, "Oh, I have so many resources. I should organise them properly!"

So, another project begins.

Sometimes I want to build something. Sometimes I want to organise information. Sometimes I simply want to experiment with a new tool and see what it can do. Programming gives me plenty of opportunities to satisfy that curiosity.

Literature: Exploring the Human Mind

Now, let's move from code to literature.

Literature is such a broad subject, but if I had to describe my particular preferences, I would say that I read quite a lot of poetry, especially dark, psychological, and complicated poetry.

And by complicated, I do not necessarily mean poetry that is difficult to understand because of its vocabulary or structure. I mean the kind of complexity that makes human life easier to understand.

If you explore the poem section of this website, you might notice a common theme. Many of the poems explore human nature, human existence, downfall, sin, pride, emotions, and the darker aspects of life.

Of course, not every poem is dark. I have my share of happy and cheerful ones too. I am not always sad, you know!

What I particularly enjoy about literature is that it allows you to explore thoughts and emotions that you might not otherwise think about. A poem can make you question something you have always believed. A story can make you understand a person whose decisions you would never have agreed with.

Psychology: But Why Do People Behave This Way?

Psychology is another subject that fascinates me. I enjoy reading about human behaviour, psychological disorders, different mental health conditions, phenomena, and syndromes.

You might come across topics such as narcissism, multiple personality disorder (now commonly referred to as dissociative identity disorder), bipolar disorder, ADHD, and several other psychological concepts. I find these subjects interesting because they introduce different ways of understanding people.

But there is one question that I find particularly fascinating:

Why?

Why does someone behave in a particular way? Why can one person be so kind while another can be so cruel? How can two people experience the same situation and react so differently?

And sometimes, the most interesting contradiction exists within the same person. Someone might be incredibly caring towards their family but behave very differently towards other people.

Human behaviour is rarely as straightforward as we would like it to be. There are emotions, experiences, circumstances, motivations, and so many other factors involved.

I don't claim to understand all of psychology. Far from it! But I love exploring these questions and learning about the different ways the human mind works.

Music: A Playlist That Makes No Sense

And then there is music. I absolutely love listening to songs of different kinds, especially those with meaningful lyrics.

My playlist, however, is a little chaotic.

At one moment, I might be listening to an extremely sad song. The next moment, I might switch to something hyper-energetic. And sometimes, there is absolutely no reason for the transition.

Why am I listening to a heartbreaking song when nothing particularly heartbreaking has happened? Good question. I have no idea!

I have even considered sharing some of my playlists on this blog. They are probably a little strange, but that is precisely what makes them interesting.

So, programming, literature, psychology, music, and several other interests. And these are only a few examples!

"Curiosity does not always have a destination. Sometimes, the joy is simply in discovering where it takes you."

2. The Excitement of Discovering Something New

Have you ever been doing something for so long that you suddenly feel the need to try something completely different?

Imagine that you have been programming for several hours. You have written code, fixed bugs, tested things, and perhaps stared at the same screen for far too long.

And then your mind says, "Enough coding. Let's do something else!"

Maybe you want to sing. Maybe you want to dance. Maybe you want to read a book, explore psychology, try crafting, or simply do something that has absolutely nothing to do with your regular work.

I think this excitement of switching interests is one of the nicest things about being curious. You get to experience the feeling of being a beginner again. There is something exciting about discovering a new subject, learning its basics, and finding out what you can do with it.

Blogging: My Little Escape

This is also one of the reasons I enjoy blogging so much. It gives me a place to put all those thoughts and ideas that keep appearing in my head.

Sometimes I have an idea and immediately create a rough draft. It might be just a few sentences, a paragraph, or a collection of random thoughts. I save it, come back to it later, improve it, organise it, and eventually publish it.

Not every idea becomes an article immediately. Some drafts stay unfinished for a while. Others turn into something completely different from what I originally imagined.

But every new blog feels like a little journey. I genuinely enjoy going back and reading my older articles. It is almost like revisiting a version of myself that existed when I wrote them.

That is why I think of blogging as a kind of escape. It gives me the freedom to explore an idea without necessarily having to turn it into something productive or practical.

Crafting: The Art of Trying Anyway

Another thing I enjoy is crafting. I have colours, papers, and plenty of ideas. The only small problem is that my execution does not always match my imagination.

I am terrible at painting. Absolutely terrible. I sometimes draw something first and then have to decide what it is supposed to represent!

Imagine that I want to draw a flower with five petals. I decide to use different colours, try to coordinate them, and make the design look beautiful. But then one petal becomes smaller than the others, the colours don't quite match, and suddenly the entire flower looks like it has been through an unfortunate design experiment.

Who made that? What was the original design? I have questions!

One of the few diagrams I remember drawing correctly was a cyclone diagram from Class 10. Yes, that complicated-looking diagram!

A cyclone diagram — one of the rare diagrams I could actually draw correctly!

My teacher was so surprised that she personally asked me who had drawn it. She was apparently convinced that I could not possibly have made it myself. I was like, "Excuse me, I did draw that!"

And honestly, that is the fun part. You might be terrible at something, but you can still enjoy doing it. You do not have to be particularly good at painting to enjoy colours. You do not have to be an excellent dancer to enjoy dancing. You do not need to publish a book to enjoy writing.

Sometimes, the excitement of trying something new is reason enough.

"You don't have to be good at everything you enjoy. Sometimes, enjoying the process is the entire point."

3. The Myth of Unfinished Projects

One of the common things people associate with having too many interests is having too many unfinished projects.

You start a course, read a few chapters, begin a website, collect ideas for a painting, and then move on to something else. Eventually, you have several things lying around that are only half done.

Now, I cannot say that this is exactly how I work. Personally, I tend to finish most of the things I start.

And before you assume that everything I make is wonderful, let me clarify something: finished does not necessarily mean perfect. It simply means that I have brought it to some kind of conclusion.

Sometimes, I finish a project even though I am not entirely happy with the result. Sometimes, I complete a design and then look at it wondering what exactly I was thinking.

But at least it is finished!

Of course, having multiple interests can sometimes mean having several things waiting for your attention: an unfinished book, a course you want to continue, a website you want to improve, or a drawing that you abandoned because you did not like how it looked.

And I don't think you should automatically feel guilty about every unfinished thing.

Not every hobby needs to become a completed project. Not every book needs to be read in a week. Not every idea needs to become a business, a portfolio piece, or a social media post.

Sometimes, you try something, learn a little, enjoy the experience, and move on. That is perfectly fine too.

The result is not always the most important part. The experience of trying, exploring, and learning can be valuable in itself.

"Not everything you start has to become a masterpiece. Sometimes, the experience is the achievement."

4. The Fear of Not Specialising: Jack of All Trades, Master of None?

Now comes the part that I genuinely struggle with: the fear of not specialising.

You have probably heard the expression "Jack of all trades, master of none." And if you have several interests, there is a good chance that you have wondered whether you are becoming exactly that.

I identify with this feeling quite a lot. Although, if you ask me what I am, I might say that I am somewhere between a jack of all trades and a person who simply likes trying things.

The concern becomes more noticeable when you look at your education and career choices.

In my case, I studied Computer Science and Engineering for my B.Tech. Programming, software development, and technology were a major part of my academic journey.

And now, what am I doing? An M.A. in English!

Don't ask me why. Ha ha!

Well, the reason is actually quite simple. I have always been interested in literature, and I wanted to explore it more seriously. Studying English literature allows me to do exactly that.

It might look like a rather unexpected transition from Computer Science to English, but I don't necessarily see these interests as opposites. Technology and literature explore different aspects of the world. One allows me to build and solve problems; the other allows me to read, interpret, question, and understand.

Still, the fear remains.

Will I ever become an expert in one field? Or will I always know a little about programming, a little about psychology, a few novels, a few programming languages, and several other things without becoming particularly specialised in any of them?

I think one approach that works well is to have one primary direction in life: an occupation, a professional ambition, or a field that receives most of your time and attention.

Your other interests can remain hobbies. They don't have to compete with your primary occupation. You can read literature even if you work in technology. You can explore psychology without becoming a psychologist. You can enjoy design without becoming a professional designer.

And whenever something catches your attention, you can make time for it. Read a book, take a course, experiment with a tool, or simply explore the subject for the joy of learning.

I don't think every interest needs to become a career choice.

"You can have one profession and a hundred passions. They don't have to fight for the same place in your life."

5. Unexpected Connections: How Different Interests Come Together

One of the most beautiful things about having different interests is discovering how they connect with one another.

At first, programming, psychology, literature, science, design, and music might seem like entirely separate subjects. But when you explore them, you begin to notice interesting overlaps.

Programming can introduce you to logical thinking and problem-solving. Psychology can help you think about human behaviour and how people interact with technology. Design can influence how you present information. Literature can help you communicate ideas and understand different perspectives.

Even something as simple as writing a blog can bring several of these interests together. You might use your technical knowledge to explain an idea, your interest in psychology to explore human behaviour, and your love for literature to make the writing more engaging.

If you have read my article If My Life Were a Git Repository, you might already understand what I mean. It started with a technical concept and gradually became a personal reflection on identity, memories, experiences, and life.

That is the beauty of connecting different ideas. Sometimes, an interest that seems completely unrelated to another one turns out to be exactly what you need to look at a problem differently.

You don't always know where your curiosity will take you. And perhaps that is the most exciting part of it.

"Sometimes, the most interesting ideas are born when two seemingly unrelated interests meet."

6. Learning to Embrace Yourself: Create Without the Pressure of Perfection

And finally, we come to what I think is the most important part: learning to embrace yourself.

I am not saying that every interest is automatically good for you, or that you should pursue every single thing that catches your attention. Time is limited, and managing your priorities is important.

But I also think that you should occasionally do something simply because you want to do it. Not because it will improve your career, make you more productive, or look impressive on your resume.

Just take a piece of paper and make something. Try painting. Make a bookmark. Experiment with colours. Write a poem. Learn a song. Do something for the simple pleasure of doing it.

My Little Bookmark Experiments

Since I mentioned crafting earlier, let me share a few bookmark ideas.

The first one is extremely simple. Take a piece of paper and fold it from the centre. Apply some glue along the sides, leaving the appropriate opening, and you have a simple handmade bookmark. Sounds crazy, but it works!

Another option is even easier: take a separate piece of paper and use it as your bookmark. Just make sure it is long enough to stick out of the book so that you can find your page later.

You can also find plenty of creative bookmark tutorials on YouTube. There are so many designs to explore!

As for me, I have ended up buying some bookmarks too. I still enjoy making my own little personal bookmarks, though.

And do you know what I like doing with them? I write dramatic quotes on them!

One of my favourite lines is:

Better to reign in Hell, than serve in Heaven.
- John Milton, Paradise Lost

I have several other quotes that I like to keep with my books. Sometimes, I add a little tassel to a bookmark. And if I don't have a ready-made tassel, I simply take an old piece of clothing, cut a small strip, and use that instead.

There is always a way to make something work with whatever materials you have.

You Don't Have to Be Good at Everything

This is something I want to emphasise.

You don't have to become an expert at every hobby. You don't have to show your creations to everyone. You don't even have to take a picture and upload it somewhere.

Make something for yourself. Keep it in a drawer. Put it inside your favourite book. Look at it after a few months and smile because you remember how much fun you had making it.

Maybe the design is terrible. Maybe the drawing is not quite what you imagined. Maybe the colours don't match. That's fine!

I often end up looking at something I have made and thinking, "Who made that? What a terrible design!"

This happens with websites too, although websites generally turn out better because I can control things like margins, padding, alignment, spacing, and boxes. There is a certain satisfaction in adjusting those properties until everything looks right.

But when you are creating something with your hands, things don't always cooperate. I cannot even draw a perfectly straight line without a ruler most of the time!

And that is okay. The point is not to produce something perfect. The point is to enjoy the experience and give yourself permission to try.

You don't need to become good at something to justify enjoying it.

"Let yourself create things that don't need to impress anyone. Some of the happiest experiences are the ones you keep entirely for yourself."

💜 Final Thoughts

So, this was my little attempt at explaining the beauty of having too many interests.

I am interested in programming, literature, psychology, music, writing, crafting, design, and several other things that I probably haven't mentioned here. And I don't necessarily want to reduce that list to just one or two interests.

Of course, I understand that time is limited. I cannot do everything at once, and I certainly cannot become an expert in every subject I find interesting. But perhaps I don't need to.

I can have a primary occupation and still enjoy several other things. I can learn something without needing to master it. I can make something imperfect and still be happy that I tried.

And perhaps that is what I want this article to say. Having many interests is not necessarily a problem that needs to be solved. It can simply be a beautiful way of experiencing the world.

I didn't write this article to convince you that being multidisciplinary automatically makes you successful, or that having multiple hobbies is some kind of superpower. I simply had this thought: I have so many interests, and that itself is something worth writing about!

Maybe you are someone who loves programming but also enjoys painting. Maybe you work in finance but spend your evenings reading about astronomy. Maybe you are a designer who loves history, or a scientist who enjoys writing poetry.

Whatever your combination is, you don't necessarily have to explain it to everyone.

Let yourself be curious. Explore something new. Give your interests some time and attention. And most importantly, don't be too hard on yourself just because you are not exceptionally good at everything you enjoy.

Some people have a calling. I seem to have an entire call centre of interests, and every single one wants my attention. And you know what? I have decided to give each of them a little time.

That's my way of managing the chaos!

"Maybe you don't need to choose just one thing to love. Maybe you just need to make room for the things that make you curious."
