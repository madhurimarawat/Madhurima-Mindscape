Embracing Failure

November 7, 2026

Failure is very important. Success is so glorified that we often overlook the very foundation that helps us understand it: failure. And I am not simply talking about huge failures, the kind where you lose everything or feel that your entire life has gone wrong. I am talking about those little mistakes that happen in everyday life.

A spelling mistake, a wrong pronunciation, an awkward answer, a presentation that refuses to load, a subject you could never understand, or something embarrassing that you remember years later and think, "Oh my God, that was so stupid!"

These little failures are also a part of growing up. They teach us things that we might never learn from books or advice alone. Sometimes, we need to experience something, make a mistake, feel the regret, and understand the lesson ourselves.

I want to talk about several such experiences and how failure, whether big or small, can actually help us become better.

"Failure is not always the opposite of success. Sometimes, it is the experience that teaches us how to understand success."

1. Public Speaking

Public speaking is one of the areas where we experience so many little failures. You are standing in front of a room full of people, already feeling anxious, and suddenly something goes wrong.

Maybe you forget a word. Maybe your pronunciation is incorrect. Maybe you say something that you did not intend to say. Your presentation refuses to load, you are unable to answer a question, or you completely lose track of what you were explaining.

The list is endless. Anyone who has given multiple presentations probably has their own collection of such embarrassing moments.

But what actually happens after such an experience? It is not always the mistake itself that stays with you. It is the feeling that comes with it.

You start thinking, "Oh my God, everyone must be thinking that I am so stupid." You remember the moment repeatedly and imagine what other people might have thought about you. Sometimes, a small mistake becomes much bigger in your mind than it ever was for the people who witnessed it.

But think about it from another perspective. At least you tried. You were anxious, you were nervous, and you still went on that stage and spoke in front of people. That itself is something worth appreciating.

Another thing is that every mistake gives you a lesson. The next time you give a presentation, you may prepare a backup copy of your slides, practise difficult pronunciations, keep notes nearby, or learn how to handle unexpected questions.

You learn because you experienced the problem. No amount of reading about public speaking can completely replace the experience of actually standing in front of an audience.

Think about walking on a path and stumbling over a stone. You fall, you get hurt, and the next time you walk along that path, you are more likely to notice the stone. That is how experience teaches us.

Of course, you may still make another mistake. That is fine. The purpose is not to become someone who never makes mistakes. It is to become someone who knows how to handle them.

"At least you tried. You stepped forward despite being anxious, and that is already a victory."

2. Learn, Then Forgive Yourself

One thing I really want to emphasise is that mistakes are excellent teachers, but we need to understand when to stop being their students.

Whenever something goes wrong, we often experience guilt, regret, embarrassment, or frustration. These feelings can help us understand what happened and encourage us to do better. But after a point, repeatedly thinking about the same mistake does not teach us anything new.

You may remember an embarrassing incident from years ago and still feel uncomfortable about it. You may think about how differently you could have handled the situation or wish that you could go back and change what happened.

But can you actually change it? No. What you can do is learn from it.

If you made a mistake while speaking, learn to communicate better. If you forgot something important, develop a habit of keeping reminders. If you hurt someone unintentionally, acknowledge it and try to make things right.

Once you have understood the lesson and done what you can to correct the situation, allow yourself to move forward. You do not need to punish yourself forever for something that happened in the past.

Guilt and regret can teach you, but they should not become permanent companions. Learn from the experience, accept that you were imperfect, and forgive yourself.

"Let your mistakes teach you, not define you. You deserve the opportunity to grow beyond them."

3. Education and Academic Failure

Education is another area where we experience different kinds of failure. Maybe you were not a very good student. Maybe you received fewer marks than expected, struggled with a particular subject, or had a lot of potential that you never really used.

Sometimes, there is a particular subject that everyone around you seems to understand easily, but you find it extremely difficult. You study it, try different methods, and still struggle to get the results you want.

And then you start comparing yourself with others. You wonder why someone else is so good at something that you find so difficult.

But here is something we need to understand: everyone has different interests, abilities, and preferences. You may dislike a particular subject, while someone else genuinely enjoys it. You may be good at something that another person struggles with.

There is no problem with being different. Not everyone needs to be equally good at everything.

Another important thing is to understand whether the particular mistake or academic failure is something you can still change or something that belongs to the past.

Suppose there is a subject you struggled with earlier, but you need that knowledge now. You can always work on it. You can revisit the concepts, practise more, and improve your understanding.

But if it is something you no longer need and it does not affect your current goals, why keep feeling bad about it? You can simply accept that you were not particularly good at that subject at that time and move forward.

There will always be people who are excellent at subjects you dislike. That is perfectly fine. They have their preferences, and you have yours.

Your marks, grades, and academic performance are parts of your journey. They do not have to become permanent labels for who you are or what you can achieve.

"You cannot change the marks you received in the past, but you can decide what you learn from that experience."

4. Career Choices and Changes

Career choices are another area where we unnecessarily associate change with failure.

Suppose you started your career in one field, perhaps Computer Science, and after spending some time there, you realised that you wanted to explore something entirely different.

Maybe you became interested in another domain. Maybe your interests changed. Maybe the work you were doing no longer excited you, or you discovered something that suited your personality and goals better.

Do not look at your earlier choice as a complete mistake. You were simply trying to figure out your way.

It is completely fine to explore different fields and discover what you genuinely enjoy. You do not need to spend your entire life following a path just because you selected it at the beginning.

If a particular field does not interest you anymore, or if you feel that it takes away your energy and leaves you unfulfilled, consider exploring other possibilities. Give yourself a chance to try something different.

Of course, career changes require planning. You may need to learn new skills, understand the opportunities available, and prepare yourself before making a major decision. But the fact that you are changing direction does not mean that your earlier journey was useless.

You gained experience, learned new things, and discovered what you like and dislike. Those lessons can help you in your next journey.

There is also another kind of failure that we rarely discuss: never trying something that you genuinely wanted to do.

Imagine that you always wanted to explore a different career, but you never gave it a chance. Years later, you may wonder what would have happened if you had tried.

Sometimes, trying something and discovering that it was not right for you is better than spending years wondering what could have happened. Even if the attempt does not work out, you will have learned something about yourself.

"Changing your direction does not mean your earlier journey was a mistake. It means you discovered a different way forward."

5. Friendships and Trust

Friendships are another important part of life where mistakes teach us a lot. Almost everyone has experienced a friendship that did not turn out the way they expected.

Maybe you trusted someone too easily. Maybe you had a friend who was around only when they needed something. Maybe someone took advantage of your kindness, used your support for their own benefit, or disappeared whenever you needed them.

Sometimes, we keep giving people chances because we genuinely want to believe that they care about us. We ignore certain behaviours, make excuses for them, and continue investing in friendships that are not healthy for us.

Later, when things go wrong, we look back and think, "Why did I trust that person so much? How could I make such a mistake?"

But I think we should look at it differently.

The fact that you trusted someone does not make you stupid. It means you were willing to build a friendship and give someone a chance. The important thing is to recognise when that trust is not being respected.

Difficult friendships can teach you how to identify certain behaviours. You gradually learn to notice when someone is being unnecessarily demanding, when they only contact you for personal benefits, or when they do not respect your boundaries.

These experiences can help you make better decisions about the people you allow into your life.

Think about it this way: if you had never experienced an unhealthy friendship, you might not recognise certain warning signs as easily. Sometimes, a bad experience helps you avoid similar situations in the future.

Of course, this does not mean you should deliberately trust people who treat you badly or keep tolerating unhealthy friendships. Learn the lesson, establish your boundaries, and move towards healthier connections.

"A difficult friendship can teach you what a healthy friendship should never make you feel."

6. Relationships and Expectations

Relationships are another area where we learn some of our most difficult lessons. We often think about the idea of the right person at the wrong time.

But I have a slightly different perspective on this.

Sometimes, we become so attached to the idea of a person or the idea of a relationship that we stop looking at the actual reality of the situation.

We imagine what the relationship could have been. We think about the possibilities, the future we pictured, and the happiness we expected to experience. Eventually, we become more attached to that imagined version than to what is actually happening.

This can make it difficult to accept that a relationship is not working. We keep thinking that perhaps things would have been different if we had done something differently or if the circumstances had been better.

But not every relationship is meant to work out. Just like friendships, relationships can become unhealthy. Someone may not respect your feelings, may not make an effort, or may expect you to keep compromising while they do very little.

When such a relationship ends, it is easy to blame yourself. You may think that you were stupid for trusting someone or that you should have understood the situation earlier.

But instead of constantly blaming yourself, try to understand what the experience taught you. Perhaps you learned the importance of communication. Perhaps you understood that affection without respect is not enough. Perhaps you discovered that you need someone who makes an effort instead of someone you constantly have to convince to stay.

I also want to question the idea of the right person at the wrong time. If someone is genuinely right for a relationship with you, their actions should reflect that. Circumstances can certainly make relationships difficult, but a healthy connection should not require you to repeatedly beg someone to stay or reduce yourself to nothing just to keep them.

The right kind of relationship allows both people to make an effort, respect each other, and choose to be present. You should not have to abandon your self-respect to maintain a relationship.

Every relationship teaches us something about what we need, what we value, and what we should not accept. Even the relationships that did not work out can help us understand what a healthy relationship should look like.

So do not keep thinking that everything would have worked out if only you had done one particular thing differently. Sometimes, accepting that something did not work out is the most important lesson.

"Do not lose yourself trying to keep someone in your life. A healthy relationship should leave room for both people to exist as themselves."

7. Understanding Success Through Failure

There is a beautiful poem by Emily Dickinson called Success Is Counted Sweetest. I particularly love this poem because it expresses something that connects perfectly with the idea of failure.

The central idea of the poem is that success is understood most deeply by those who have never experienced it. Someone who has always been victorious may not fully understand how valuable success can be. But someone who has experienced defeat understands its importance much more deeply.

The poem uses the image of a defeated soldier listening to the victorious army celebrating. The soldier who has lost understands the meaning of victory in a way that those celebrating may never fully appreciate.

This is why I love this poem. It captures the relationship between failure and success so beautifully.

Think about it. When everything comes easily, we may not understand how much effort, patience, and perseverance are involved in achieving something. But when we struggle, make mistakes, experience setbacks, and finally accomplish something, the feeling is different.

You understand the value of that achievement because you know what it took to reach it. You remember the difficult days, the unsuccessful attempts, the moments when you wanted to give up, and all the things you learned along the way.

Failure can make success more meaningful because it gives us a different perspective. It teaches us not to take achievements for granted.

And this is not limited to major achievements. Even a small improvement can feel incredibly satisfying when you remember how difficult it was for you earlier.

"Sometimes, the people who have experienced defeat understand the value of victory more deeply than those who have always won."

8. Every Failure Has a Lesson

If you look closely, failure is present in almost every part of our lives. It may be a presentation that did not go well, an examination where you received fewer marks, a career choice that did not work out, a friendship that ended, or a relationship that taught you a difficult lesson.

Some mistakes are small and embarrassing. Others can be emotionally difficult and may take a long time to understand. But both kinds of experiences can teach us something.

The important thing is to distinguish between what you can improve and what you need to accept.

If you can correct something, take responsibility and work on it. If you cannot change what happened, understand the lesson and allow yourself to move forward. There is no point in spending your entire life regretting something that is already over.

Also, remember that not every failure needs to become a life-changing lesson. Sometimes you simply make a silly mistake, laugh about it later, and continue with your day. That is fine too.

We do not need to turn every embarrassing moment into a serious discussion about personal growth. Sometimes being human means making mistakes, feeling awkward, learning something, and moving on.

"You are allowed to make mistakes, learn from them, and still be kind to yourself."

💜 Final Thoughts

Success is celebrated everywhere, but failure deserves recognition too. It is through our mistakes, setbacks, embarrassing moments, and difficult experiences that we gradually understand ourselves and the world around us.

I am not saying that failure is always enjoyable or that every difficult experience is automatically good for us. Some experiences genuinely hurt. Some mistakes have consequences, and some situations take time to recover from. But even then, we can eventually learn something from them.

Do not be afraid of making mistakes while trying something new. Do not consider a career change a failure simply because you changed your mind. Do not blame yourself forever for trusting the wrong person. And do not allow one embarrassing presentation or one disappointing examination to define your abilities.

Learn what you can, make changes where necessary, and forgive yourself for the things you cannot change. You are not supposed to get everything right the first time.

And if you ever feel that failure makes you less capable of achieving something, remember Emily Dickinson's Success Is Counted Sweetest. Sometimes, experiencing defeat is precisely what helps us understand the value of success.

So make mistakes, try new things, explore different paths, and do not be unnecessarily harsh on yourself. You may look back at some of your past experiences and laugh. Others may remind you of how much you have grown.

"You do not have to be someone who never fails. Be someone who keeps learning, growing, and trying again."
